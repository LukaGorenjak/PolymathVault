### Key Economic Indicators to Track

| **Indicator**                    | **What It Measures**                           | **Why It Matters**                                                                                      |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Real GDP**                     | Total economic output (adjusted for inflation) | Shows overall growth or shrinkage.                                                                      |
| **Unemployment & Payrolls**      | Job market health                              | Hiring rises in growth, drops in contractions.                                                          |
| **Consumer Spending & Income**   | How much people earn and spend                 | Consumption drives ~70% of modern economies.                                                            |
| **Industrial Production**        | Factory, utility, and mining output            | Responds quickly to changes in demand.                                                                  |
| **Interest Rates & Yield Curve** | Cost of borrowing / Bond market signals        | Central banks adjust rates to manage cycles; an inverted yield curve often signals upcoming recessions. |
1. **Expansion**
	- **GDP:** Growing steadily (typically above 2%).
	- **Labor Market:** Unemployment low and falling; job opening are high.
	- **Spending & Business:** Retail sales are strong; companies investing in expansion.
	- **Monetary Policy:** Interest rates typically stable or gradually rising as the economy grows.
2. **Peak**
	- **GDP:** Growth reaches its highest level and starts to slow or stall.
	- **Labor Market:** Unemployment reaches historic lows; wage growth is high, leading to labor shortages.
	- **Spending & Business:** Prices often rise rapidly ([[Overheating]]).
	- **Monetary Policy:** Central banks raise interest rates aggressively to cool down inflation.
 3. **Contraction (Recession)**
	- **GDP:** Negative or near-zero growth for multiple quarters.
	- **Labor Market:** Unemployment starts rising; hiring freezes and layoffs increase.
	- **Spending & Business:** Consumers cut discretionary spending; corporate profits decline.
	- **Monetary Policy:** Central banks begin cutting interest rates to encourage borrowing and stimulate growth.
 4. **Trough**
	- **GDP:** Decline stops, output hits bottom, and stabilizes.
	- **Labor Market:** Unemployment peaks, then starts to flatten out.
	- **Spending & Business:** Business inventories are cleared out; consumer sentiment begins to turn positive.
	- **Monetary Policy:** Interest rates are at or near their lowest levels, making borrowing very cheap.
