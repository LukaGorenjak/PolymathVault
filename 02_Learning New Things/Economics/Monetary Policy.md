## ==🟢Overview==
- **Definition:** Policy adopted by a country's monetary authority to manage financial conditions and achieve macro objectives (e.g., high employment, price stability/low inflation).
- **Secondary Goals:** Economic stability, predictable exchange rates.
- **Key Difference from Fiscal Policy:** Monetary policy uses financial channels (rates, asset prices). Fiscal policy uses taxation and government spending.
- **Independence:** Modern central banks in developed countries operate independently of government control.

## Core Strategies
- **Inflation Targeting:** Primary approach in developed countries.
- **Fixed Exchange Rate:** Primary approach in most developing countries.
- **Money Supply Targeting:** Popular in the 1980s; still used in some emerging economies.

## Key Tools & Stances
- **Primary Tool:** Interest-rate targeting (set directly or via open market operations).
- **Other Tools:** Reserve requirements, communication strategies (forward guidance).
- **Policy Stances:**
    - **Expansionary:** Lowers interest rates $\rightarrow$ stimulates growth, employment, and inflation.
    - **Contractionary:** Raises interest rates $\rightarrow$ cools growth, decreases employment and inflation.
