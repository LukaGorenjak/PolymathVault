Transactions the building blocks of economics the sum of these is the economy.
Transaction is between buyer and seller (credit/money for goods/services/financial assets) and this drives the economy.
Price = Total spending (credit + money) / Total quantity

A market contains all the buyers and sellers making transactions for the same thing- (Fish, car, stock, wheat markets)
total spending / total quantity 

The biggest buyer and seller is the government. Consisting of: 
- Central government (collects taxes and spends money)
- Central bank (controls amount of money/credit in economy). Does this by influencing interest rates and printing new money.

Credit is the most important part of the economy. Just like transactions here its between lenders and borrowers. Borrowers promise to pay back the principal + interest. When interest rates are high there is less borrowing because it's expensive and opposite.
As soon as credit is created there is also debt, which is an asset for the lender and a liability for the borrower.
When a borrower recieves credit he is able to increase his spending which drives the economy. Beacuse one's spending is another's income.
A creditworthy borrower has 2 things:
- The ability to repay
- Collateral if he can't

So there is a cycle:
- INCOME --> BORROWING --> SPENDING --> PRODUCTIVITY -->
- This self-reinforcing pattern leads to economic growth and is why we have cycles. 

Borrowing simply is a way of pulling spending forward and borrowing from your future self. You create a time in the future where you will have to spend less than you make in order to pay it back. Which resembles a cycle, this goes same for individual or the economy.

The total amount of credit in the US is around $50T and the amount of money only about $3T.

An economy without credit only rises through productivity but with it it allows more spending by borrowing and allows incomes to rise faster than productivity over the short term but not over the long run.

Credit is bad when it finances over-consumption (new big TV) but good when it allocates resources that produce income (tractor) so you can pay it back.

The short term debt cycle (5-8 years)
	As economic activity increases we see expansion, spending continues to increase and prices start to rise (fueled by credit created out of thin air). When the amount of spending and incomes grow faster than the production of goods prices rise (inflation).
	Central banks don't want a too high inflation so they raise interest rates, so fewer people can afford to borrow and costs of existing credits rises. 
	So the person with debt has to pay more meaning he has less money to spend, which is another person's income.
	When people spend less prices go down we call this deflation. Economic activity decreases and we have a recession and if it becomes too severe and inflation is no longer a problem the central bank will lower interest rates to do it all over again.
	It's human nature when borrowing to spend more  instead of paying back debt. ----------> Long term debt cycle

Long term debt cycle
	Despite people becoming more indebted, lenders even more freely extend credit, because people live in the latest moments (more income, more valuable assets, more borrowing) we call this a bubble.
	Debt burden (debt income ratio), as long as incomes continue to rise the debt burden stays manageable. At the same time asset values soar and people borrow more to buy them as investment causing their prices to rise higher.
	But this can't continue forever. now the economy begins Deleveraging

Deleveraging
	People cut spending, incomes fall, credit disappears, asset price drop, banks get squeezed, stock market crashes, social tensions rise. Because they cannot repay debt with borrowing (not creditworthy) they are forced to sell assets floods the market, the same time that spending falls. This is when the stock market, real estate collapses and banks get into trouble. As asset prices drop the value of the collateral borrowers can put up drops this makes borrowers even less creditworthy.

Less spending --> Less income --> Less wealth--> Less credit--> Less borrowing --> (A vicious cycle) difference between this and a recessions is that interest rates can't be lowered to save the day.

Lenders stop lending, borrowers stop borrowing, think of the economy as being not-creditworthy.

4 ways to affect it:
1. Cut spending (Individuals, government, companies) (austerity)
2. Reduce debt (through defaults or reconstructing)
3. Redistribute wealth (from the 'haves' to the 'have nots')
