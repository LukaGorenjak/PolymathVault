## ==🔵Key takeaways==
- Cyclical stocks are tied to economic cycles, rising during growth and falling in recessions
- They commonly include industries such as automotive, airlines, and retailers.
- They offer potential for high returns, but they come with increased volatility.
- Investors can balance their portfolios with noncyclical stocks for stability.
- Exchange-traded funds (ETFs) provide diversified exposure to cyclical sectors.

# What is a Cyclical Stock?
Cyclical stocks rise and fall in sync with the economic cycle. They often see increased sales as consumers spend more during expansion, but their stock prices also plummet as spending contracts during recessions.

Example companies:
- Car manufacturers
- Airlines,
- Furniture retailers,
- Clothing stores,
- Hotels,
- Restaurants...

But the expenses produces by these are the first to be cut when an economy does poorly by the people, in extreme recession these can become worthless.

Investors should use caution about the weight of cyclical stocks in their portfolios at any point in time but this doesn't mean investors should steer clear of these stocks completely.

---
# Risk and Benefits
**Risks:**
- more volatile
**Benefits:**
- great potential for growth

Investors seeking long-term growth with managed volatility tend to balance their portfolios with a mix of cyclical and non-cyclical (defensive) stocks.

---
# Comparing Cyclical and Defensive Stocks
One goes with the economy, the other tends to beat the market regardless of economic trend.

Defensive stocks encompass the consumer staples category with goods and services that people continue buying through all types of economic cycles, even downturns.

Examples:
- Food,
- Gas,
- Water

# Division
Cyclical stocks are further divided into:
1. **Durables** - manufacturing or distribution of physical goods of a lifespan of more than 3 years. 
   Examples: Ford, appliance manufacturer Whirlpool, furniture maker Ethan Allen
   The measure of these goods is an indicator of stronger economic activity in the ensuing months.
2. **Nondurables** - produce or distribute soft goods of a lifespan of fewer than 3 years.
   Examples: Coca-Cola
3. **Services** - provide services that facilitate travel, entertainment and other leisure activities for consumers.
   Examples: Walt-Disney, Netflix
