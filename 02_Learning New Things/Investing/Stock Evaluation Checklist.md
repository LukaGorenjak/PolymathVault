## 1. Catalyst and Initial Context
- [ ] Check recent price action around quarterly earnings or major news.
- [ ] Compare headline results against expectations (EPS, CapEx, Free Cash Flow, core segment growth).
- [ ] Separate short-term market panic from long-term fundamentals: is a sell-off justified, or is it a buying opportunity?

## 2. Business Model and Revenue Mechanics
- [ ] Understand what the company sells (physical and digital products) and who its customers are (consumers, SMBs, enterprises).
- [ ] Break down the company's reporting segments (core cash cow, high-growth segment, experimental/other bets).
- [ ] Identify how each segment generates revenue (direct sales, fixed leases, consumption-based fees, subscriptions, advertising).
- [ ] Check business-specific KPIs and unit economics rather than just headline revenue:
  - Example: volume/engagement growth vs. price/cost growth (e.g., paid clicks vs. cost per click; impressions vs. cost per impression).
  - Verify whether customer engagement is accelerating faster than customer costs (value delivery) and flag shrinking/lagging sub-segments.

## 3. Financial Statements (Latest Quarterly and YTD Filings)

### Income Statement (Year-over-Year)
- [ ] **Top-line revenue:** Check total growth and segment-by-segment growth (which segment is accelerating vs. slowing?).
- [ ] **Cost of revenues:** Review third-party/partner fees, infrastructure costs, depreciation & amortization (D&A), inventory carrying costs, and direct technical labor.
- [ ] **Operating expenses:**
  - **R&D:** Engineer compensation, infrastructure D&A, energy/capacity overhead, new product development.
  - **Sales & Marketing:** Headcount, commissions, promotional activity.
  - **G&A:** Admin/legal/IT/HR costs and one-off legal settlements or fines.
- [ ] **Operating income and margin:** Confirm whether core operations are improving YoY.
- [ ] **Other income/expenses and Net Income:** Check interest income/expense, FX gains/losses, and mark-to-market gains/losses on equity stakes (ensure net margin swings aren't misread as operational changes).

### Balance Sheet (Period-over-Period)
- [ ] **Liquidity:** Track cash, cash equivalents, and marketable securities (is the company building a cash cushion to fund heavy CapEx?).
- [ ] **Long-term assets:** Check PP&E growth (data centers, equipment, infrastructure) and non-marketable securities (private investments).
- [ ] **Current liabilities:** Watch accounts payable and accrued liabilities (a sharp rise can indicate delayed vendor payments to protect cash).
- [ ] **Debt and Equity:** Check increases in long-term debt, new common/preferred stock issuances (dilution), retained earnings, and stockholders' equity.
- [ ] **Off-balance-sheet commitments:** Look for Joint Ventures (JVs), Special Purpose Vehicles (SPVs), Variable Interest Entities (VIEs), future CapEx/lease commitments, and financial backstops/guarantees for smaller partners or customers. Account for hidden obligations not shown on the face of the balance sheet.

### Statement of Cash Flows (Year-over-Year)
- [ ] **Operating Cash Flow (OCF):** Inspect non-cash adjustments (D&A, stock-based compensation, deferred taxes/tax harvesting, investment gains/losses) and working capital changes (receivables, inventory, payables).
- [ ] **Investing Activities:** Track CapEx (PP&E purchases), investments in private companies/JVs, and marketable securities flows.
- [ ] **Free Cash Flow (FCF):** Calculate `OCF - CapEx`. If FCF is negative, determine if it is temporary growth investment and when it turns positive.
- [ ] **Financing Activities:** Check whether share buybacks paused, and how much capital was raised via new debt vs. new stock issuances.

## 4. Qualitative Assessment (SWOT Analysis)
- [ ] **Strengths:** Competitive moat, product ecosystem stickiness, market share, high-growth divisions.
- [ ] **Weaknesses:** Lagging or declining business lines, rising overhead, lack of focus / opportunity cost from spreading resources across too many areas.
- [ ] **Opportunities:** User adoption scale, product/tech benchmark leads, supply-constrained demand, order backlog, vertical integration / cost savings (e.g., in-house hardware vs. third-party reliance).
- [ ] **Threats:** Overextension or overleveraging on heavy CapEx without proven ROI; regulatory, legislative, or political risks (e.g., post-election policy shifts).

## 5. Market Snapshot and Key Metrics
- [ ] Current share price and recent trading range.
- [ ] Average daily volume (liquidity).
- [ ] Market capitalization and total public float (include all share classes/tickers).
- [ ] Earnings Per Share (EPS) and Price-to-Earnings (P/E) ratio.
- [ ] Dividend yield and dividend per share.
- [ ] **Beta:** Measure of volatility compared to the broader market.
- [ ] **Short interest:** Total shares shorted and percentage of public float.

## 6. Intrinsic Valuation (DCF Analysis)
- [ ] Gather baseline inputs: 10-year Treasury yield (risk-free rate), required investor return, beta, weighted average interest rate on debt, total debt, market cap, and cash.
- [ ] Calculate **WACC** (Weighted Average Cost of Capital) and choose a conservative **terminal growth rate**.
- [ ] Forecast multi-year revenue, margins, operating expenses (including future D&A from current CapEx), net income, OCF, and CapEx using historical trends and management guidance.
- [ ] Identify when peak CapEx tapers off and when AI/growth investments begin generating positive FCF.
- [ ] Compute **Enterprise Value** (sum of discounted FCF + terminal value), then **Implied Equity Value** (`Enterprise Value - Debt + Cash`), and **Implied Share Price**.
- [ ] Check the projected annualized return over your holding period (including dividends).
- [ ] Apply a risk discount if forecast assumptions are optimistic or if off-balance-sheet risks are high.

## 7. Technical Analysis (Entry Timing)
- [ ] Review price action over a recent window (e.g., 90 days on a 1-hour chart) to identify current **support and resistance** levels.
- [ ] Check volume and money flow indicators:
  - **OBV (On-Balance Volume):** Look for uptrends or stabilization after a sell-off.
  - **CMF (Chaikin Money Flow):** Watch for buying reversals.
  - **Accumulation/Distribution:** Distinguish normal earnings spikes from sustained trends.
- [ ] Check momentum with **RSI (Relative Strength Index)** (oversold, neutral, or overbought).
- [ ] Check price action quality (bid-ask/open-close dispersion and volume).

## 8. Final Decision
- [ ] Weigh business quality and growth against balance-sheet and off-balance-sheet risks.
- [ ] Set a risk-adjusted **target price**.
- [ ] Decide: **Buy, Hold, or Sell** (and define your acceptable entry price or support level).
