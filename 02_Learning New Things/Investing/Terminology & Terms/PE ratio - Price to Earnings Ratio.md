# What is it?
Financial metric that compares a company's current share price to its [[EPS - earnings per share]]. It tells how much investors are willing to pay for each dollar a company earns.

# How to interpret it?
- **High P/E Ratio:** Can mean the stock is **overvalued**, or it can signal that investors expect **strong future growth** and are paying a premium for the company's potential (common in tech or high-growth sectors).
- **Low P/E Ratio:** Can mean the stock is a **bargain (undervalued)**, or it can indicate the company is facing **trouble, weak growth, or a declining outlook**.
- **Negative or Zero P/E:** If a company has a net loss (no positive earnings), its P/E ratio is typically labeled as **N/A**.
- **General rule of thumb:** 15 - 25 considered healthy.
