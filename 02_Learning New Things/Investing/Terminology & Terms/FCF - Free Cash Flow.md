# What is FCF and understanding it?
- Represents the cash a company generates after covering [[OpEx - Operating Expense]] and [[CapEx - Capital Expenditure]]. So the money it has left after spending to support and maintain its operations (everyday running costs) and capital assets (big, long-term items).
- Investors and analysts rely on it as a key measure of a company's financial flexibility and underlying profitability.
- Unlike earnings or net income, FCF excludes the [[Non-Cash Expenses]] of company's income statement. Includes CapEx such as PP&E - Property, Plant and Equipment, changes in working capital. Making it more favorable for some investors.
- Free cash flow is the money a company has available to repay creditors, pay dividends, reduce debt, or reinvest in the business.
- PP&E accounting can make FCF lumpy and uneven over time, YoY comparisons more volatile and potentially misleading without context.
